# Baseline analysis — what the Phase 1 numbers mean

`12-eval-results.md` is generated by the harness and holds the numbers. This file holds the
reasoning about them, and is written by hand. Keeping them apart is deliberate: a generated
file cannot carry argument, and an argument that lives inside a generated file gets deleted
by the next run.

Written 2026-09-15 against the first harness run: 3 GAMUS val tiles, all Washington DC,
Depth Anything V2 Base, frozen, zero-shot, no calibration.

---

## The headline, and why the headline is misleading

Pooled over all pixels, correlation with LiDAR height is **+0.037**. That reads as: the
off-the-shelf backbone does nothing useful on overhead imagery.

Stratified, it is not that at all:

| Scope | r | SI-RMSE | pixels |
|---|---|---|---|
| All pixels | +0.037 | 8.82 m | 3,145,728 |
| **Building** | **+0.156** | 3.92 m | 520,250 |
| Tree | −0.000 | 8.43 m | 1,532,788 |
| Road | −0.060 | 5.69 m | 552,560 |
| Ground | +0.031 | 0.46 m | 295,123 |

**Trees are 49% of the pixels and carry the largest error.** The pooled figure is mostly a
measurement of how badly the model does on canopy, with the building signal averaged into
invisibility.

## What the model is actually doing

It finds buildings and misses vegetation.

`out/baseline_comparison.png` shows this without any statistics. The predicted panel picks
out building footprints as raised rectangles in roughly the right places. The reference
panel is dominated by tree crowns at ~17 m that the prediction leaves flat.

The correlation is weak even on buildings (+0.156), so this is not a good result. But it is
a *directional* one: there is signal on built structure to sharpen, rather than nothing to
work with.

## Why vegetation fails here

The imagery is **leaf-off winter**. Bare deciduous canopy is brown, not green, with ground
texture visible through the branches. A 17 m tree that presents as twigs over bare earth
offers nothing a model trained on summer street-level photography would read as an elevated
surface.

**This is untested on leaf-on or evergreen imagery, and it matters.** If the failure is
seasonal, forest performance on Indian imagery may be very different — in either direction.
If it is fundamental — canopy is diffuse and has no crisp upper surface to latch onto — then
the forest limitation is structural and needs stating in the submission rather than fixed.
Resolving this needs leaf-on data, which we do not have (D-02).

## Two explanations ruled out

**A sign-convention bug on our side.** Depth Anything emits inverse depth, and if we had the
sign backwards every correlation would be negative. Two findings rule it out: the pooled
sign swings between tiles of the same city (−0.27 to +0.21), and building correlation is
positive on all three tiles while tree correlation is not. An inverted convention would flip
both together.

**The model reporting albedo rather than height.** Plausible here, because on leaf-off
imagery the tall canopy is dark and the bare ground is bright, so brightness anti-correlates
with height. Measured and rejected:

| Tile | r(pred, AGL) | r(pred, brightness) | r(brightness, AGL) |
|---|---|---|---|
| DC_02_26 | −0.2677 | +0.1705 | −0.2028 |
| DC_04_23 | +0.2126 | −0.0931 | +0.0461 |
| DC_04_27 | +0.1662 | −0.0202 | −0.1633 |

Correlation with brightness is as weak as correlation with height. The model is not
substituting one for the other.

## What this changes

1. **Hard rule 8 now has evidence, not just principle.** Scoring forest separately was
   adopted because prediction and reference measure different surfaces over canopy. This is
   the first measurement of what pooling costs: it turns +0.156 on buildings into +0.037
   overall.

2. **Building-only metrics are the honest headline for built-up scenes**, and the brief's
   disaster-management framing is about structures. Report both, lead with the stratified
   one.

3. **Fine-tuning has a foundation.** A backbone at r ≈ 0 everywhere would mean creating a
   signal from nothing. One at +0.156 on buildings is being asked to sharpen an existing
   one, which is a much better position.

4. **Road at −0.060 is worth a second look.** Roads are flat and at 0 m AGL by definition,
   so there is almost no true height variation for a correlation to track — the figure may
   be close to meaningless rather than a failure. SI-RMSE of 5.69 m on a surface that should
   be flat is the more telling number, and suggests the model is putting relief where there
   is none.

## The caveat that outweighs all of the above

**Three tiles, one city, one season.** They were chosen only because they were the first to
finish downloading. Nothing here is a finding yet; it is a direction. The numbers will move
when the harness runs over a real held-out split, and the leave-one-city-out split
(`eval/scenes.py`) has never been exercised because only DC tiles exist locally.
